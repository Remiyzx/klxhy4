Culture Corner: How Do We Greet People? KLXHY pg 20

Classroom Discussion & Warm-up Question:

How do you greet a stranger / elder / senior vs. a close friend / acquaintance in our multicultural society?

In Local Malay Culture:
  • Religious / Elders: "Assalamualaikum" (with salam hand gesture).
  • General / Formal: "Selamat pagi", "Selamat petang".
  • Daily Casual (Friends): "Pergi mana tu?" (Where are you heading?), "Dah makan?" (Have you eaten?), "Sihat?" / "Apa khabar?".
In Sabah Local Culture (Kadazan & Sabah Malay):
  • Kadazan Traditional: "Kopisanangan" (Hello / Greetings), "Kopisanangan kosuabon" (Good morning).
  • Sabah Daily Slang (Campus): "Pi mana kau?" / "Mau pigi mana tu?", "Makan sudah kah?", "Sihat bah kau?".
Cultural Insight for Chinese Greetings:

Notice something familiar? Just like local Malaysians asking "Pergi mana?" or "Makan sudah?", Chinese people asking acquaintances "Qù nǎr?" (Where are you going?) or "Chī le ma?" (Have you eaten?) is NOT being rude or nosy—it is just a friendly, warm way to say hello and show care!

Teacher's Tips: Choosing the Right Greeting

Zǎoshang hǎo

Conversational spoken style. Follows the natural daily pattern: [Time Period] + hǎo. Used most commonly in daily verbal chats.

Zǎo'ān

Formal & ceremonial style. Often heard in morning announcements, school assemblies, broadcasts, or polite written messages.

Wǎnshang hǎo

"Good evening!" Used when meeting or greeting someone during the evening or night hours.

Wǎn'ān

"Good night!" Strictly used when saying goodbye, parting ways at night, or right before going to sleep.

Ways to Say "Sorry": Choose the Right One!

  • Duìbuqi: "I'm sorry" (Standard, direct apology).
    Used when you make a clear mistake, cause hurt, or break something (e.g., breaking a cup, stepping on someone's foot).
  • Bù hǎo yìsi: "Excuse me / My bad / Feeling shy" (Mild & polite).
    Much softer and casual. Used when passing through a crowd ("excuse me"), asking a small favor, or feeling politely shy/grateful when treated to food.
  • Bàoqiàn: "We deeply apologize" (Formal & official).
    Used in official announcements, business notices, flight delays, or formal customer service.

Negation Pattern with "bù" / "bú"

Tone Sandhi Rules for "bù" (MUST KNOW) KLXHY pg 36

To negate an action or state, place bù (or bú) directly before the verb: S + bù/bú + V + O.

Tone Sandhi Rules for "bù" (Textbook Reference: KLXHY pg 36):
  • Rule 1: "bù" → "bú" (Tone Change before 4th Tone Syllable)
    When followed by a 4th-tone syllable (like zài, qù, yào, kè), "bù" changes to 2nd tone "bú"!
    Examples: bú zài (not at), bú qù (not going), bú yào (don't want), bú kèqi (you're welcome).
  • Rule 2: "bù" remains 4th Tone "bù"
    Before 1st, 2nd, or 3rd-tone syllables (like chī, hē, xǐhuan, huí), it stays original 4th tone "bù".
    Examples: bù chī (don't eat), bù hē (don't drink), bù xǐhuan (don't like), bù huí jiā (not going home).

Important Rule: Question Words vs "ma"

Golden Rule: NEVER combine "ma" with 5W1H Question Words!

If a sentence already contains a 5W1H question word like "nǎr / nǎlǐ" (where) or "shénme" (what), do NOT add "ma" at the end!

  • Correct: Nǐ qù nǎr? / Nǐ chī shénme?
  • Incorrect: Nǐ qù nǎr ma? / Nǐ chī shénme ma?

ATTENTION: "qù" vs "huí jiā"

Do NOT combine "qù" with "jiā" to say "go home"!

  • Instead, say huí jiā (return home).
  • Use "huí" when returning to your own place (e.g., huí jiā, huí sùshè).

Grammar Note: Time Word Position in Chinese

In Chinese, time words (e.g., jīntiān, míngtiān, xīngqī..., ...diǎn...fēn) MUST always be placed BEFORE the action verb!
You can place the time word either after the subject (Pattern 1) or at the very front (Pattern 2):

• In English, time often sits at the end: "I go to the library today."

Grammar Note: Place Word Position in Chinese

In Chinese, the Place (Location Phrase) MUST be placed BEFORE the action verb!
Place phrases are formed with zài (at), qù (go to), or huí (return to) + Location.

Subject (S)
(Siapa)		 Time
(Bila)		 Place Phrase
(zài / qù / huí + Location)		 Verb (V)
(Buat)		 Object (O)
(Apa)		 Meaning
(English)
Wǒ jīntiān zài cāntīng chī hànbāo I eat hamburger at the restaurant today.
Wǒmen míngtiān qù diànyǐngyuàn kàn diànyǐng We go to the cinema to watch movie tomorrow.
Tā xīngqīsān huí jiā chī píngguǒ He returns home to eat apples on Wednesday.
Lǎoshī sān diǎn zài bàngōngshì hē kāfēi Teacher drinks coffee at the office at 3 o'clock.
English vs. Chinese Word Order Pitfall:
• English order: "I eat hamburger at the restaurant" (Action + Place).
• Chinese order: "Wǒ zài cāntīng chī hànbāo" (Place + Action).

9. Lesson 4 Summary & Takeaways

1. Everyday Greetings & Nuances
  • Casual/Everyday: Nǐ hǎo (Hello), Lǎoshī hǎo (Hello teacher).
  • Time-Specific: Zǎoshang hǎo (Morning), Xiàwǔ hǎo (Afternoon), Wǎnshang hǎo (Evening).
  • Formal vs. Daily: Zǎo'ān is more formal/broadcast; Wǎn'ān strictly means "Good night / sleep well".
  • Responses & Extensions:
    • Xièxie (nǐ/nǐmen) → Bú kèqi.
    • Zàijiàn (Míngtiān/Xīngqīsān jiàn).
    • Duìbuqi → Méi guānxi (contrast with mild Bù hǎo yìsi & formal Bàoqiàn).
2. Two Vital Grammar Particles
  • Question Particle "ma":
    • Turns any statement into a yes-no question:
    • (S + Adjective) + ma? → Nǐ hǎo ma?
    • (S + V + O) + ma? → Nǐ chī hànbāo ma?
  • Plural Marker "men":
    • Exclusively for people/pronouns: wǒmen (we), nǐmen (you all), tāmen (they), lǎoshīmen (teachers).
    • Never used for objects, places, or animals!
3. The Master Word Order Formula
Subject + Time + Place (zài/qù/huí) + Verb + Object
  • Time Position: Must come before the verb (S + Time + V or Time + S + V).
  • Place Position: Always placed BEFORE the action verb!
    • English: I eat at restaurant.
    • Chinese: Wǒ zài cāntīng chī.
4. Four Critical Golden Rules
  • Return Home: Never say qù jiā; ALWAYS say huí jiā! (Use huí for your own place, qù for other places).
  • "bù" Tone Sandhi: Changes to 2nd tone "bú" before a 4th tone syllable (bú zài, bú qù, bú yào, bú kèqi). Stays 4th tone "bù" before 1st/2nd/3rd tones (bù chī, bù hē, bù huí jiā).
  • Question Words: Never add "ma" when using nǎr / nǎlǐ (where) or shénme (what)!
  • Location Suffixes: Recognize building types by suffixes: -xué, -shè, -shì, -tīng, -guǎn, -yuàn, -chǎng.
